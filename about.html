About

The short version.

I'm a numbers guy who happens to run real estate companies, not a real estate guy who happens to know numbers. That distinction is at the core of everything I've built.

Pittsburgh comes first

Pittsburgh comes first.

I grew up in Mount Washington and have spent most of my life in Pittsburgh. I went to Canevin High School and then Duquesne University, where I studied accounting and played tight end until a career-ending injury ended my football career.

After college, I became a CPA and spent roughly a decade in the profession. I spent seven or eight years in audit at Ernst & Young, working with companies ranging from Fortune 500 businesses to small startups, followed by finance transformation consulting at Grant Thornton that had me traveling nearly 70% of the time.

The turning point

The turning point.

In 2018, I took a significant pay cut to become CFO of a local Pittsburgh real estate company. It was my first real exposure to the industry, and I spent about ten months restructuring the company's back office.

Then I was fired.

I had just gotten married, had a young family, and had spent ten years building expertise that I didn't want to put back on a corporate résumé. I told my wife I needed six months to figure something out.

I started doing bookkeeping for real estate agents for $20 an hour. In May 2019, I put together the capital for my first deal: a $40,000 single-family home in Allentown, Pittsburgh.

What came next

What came next.

That first property became the beginning of CityLife Residential. From there, I built a lending company after seeing good Pittsburgh investors get rejected by lenders who didn't understand the neighborhoods they were investing in. I built Accruity after repeatedly meeting business owners making major decisions with financial information they couldn't trust. Provato grew from the same pattern: identifying a problem and building a better solution.

What connects the businesses isn't simply real estate. It's disciplined capital structuring. I'm interested in where money goes, why it goes there, how it gets managed, and what happens when the original plan changes.

What I've learned

What I've learned.

Numbers tell you what happened. They don't tell you what will happen.

I learned that by operating. I built a wholesale real estate division to $1.2M in its first year, then shut it down 18 months later when it became clear it wasn't the business I wanted to run. I don't consider that a failure. If you weren't wiped out, you didn't lose. It's just a round.

That experience reinforced something I still believe: discipline matters more than momentum. Knowing when to keep going matters. Knowing when to walk away matters, too.

Pittsburgh is still home.

My wife, Kelsey, and I are raising three kids here. I've built my businesses here, invested here, hired people from here, and continued to put capital back into the communities that shaped me.
